How to stop hitting snooze

You set the alarm with good intentions. Then the half-asleep version of you makes a different decision, and makes it nine minutes at a time. Here is why that happens and what helps.

Why snooze is so hard to resist

When an alarm goes off you are in the groggiest minutes of your day. That fog has a name, sleep inertia, and it is when judgment and willpower are at their lowest. The snooze button offers an instant reward (the noise stops, the bed is warm) for one small tap. The cost comes later, so the tap wins.

The extra minutes are not good sleep, either. They are short and broken, and many people feel worse after three snoozes than they would have getting up at the first alarm.

So the fix is rarely more willpower. It is setting things up so the easy choice and the right choice are the same.

Seven things that work

  1. Remove the snooze button. If the option is not there, you can't take it. Most phone clocks let you switch snooze off per alarm, and a no-snooze alarm app never offers it at all.
  2. Put the phone out of reach. Across the room, or in the hallway. Standing up is most of the battle.
  3. Get into bright light straight away. Light is the strongest signal your body gets that the day has started. Open the curtains or turn on the main room light, not just a bedside lamp.
  4. Set one alarm for the real time. A row of alarms ten minutes apart is snooze with extra steps. Pick the latest time that still works and set only that.
  5. Give yourself something to get up for. Coffee on a timer, a podcast you only play in the morning, ten quiet minutes before anyone else is up. A small reward beats a big threat.
  6. Keep the same wake-up time. Including weekends, within an hour or so. A steady time makes waking easier within a couple of weeks.
  7. Go to bed earlier. Not a trick, but nothing above fixes a real shortage of sleep. If mornings are a fight every day, look at your bedtime first.

What a no-snooze alarm does differently

A normal alarm asks you to tap the screen. A no-snooze alarm asks you to prove you are up. Some make you solve sums, scan a barcode in the bathroom or shake the phone. Each one pulls you a little further out of sleep before the sound stops.

If you still can't get up

Give any change a week or two before judging it. If you sleep seven to nine hours and still can't wake, or you regularly sleep through loud alarms, it is worth talking to a doctor. Trouble waking can be a sign of a sleep problem rather than a habit.
