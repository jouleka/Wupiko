Alarms for heavy sleepers: what actually gets you out of bed

If you sleep through alarms, or switch them off without remembering it, a louder ringtone will not fix it. What helps is an alarm you can't finish from bed.

Why loud isn't enough

Heavy sleepers usually do hear the alarm. The problem is the next ten seconds: a hand finds the phone, taps whatever makes the noise stop, and sleep takes over again. Often there is no memory of it.

So the useful question is not "how loud is it?" but "what do I have to do to turn it off, and can I do that asleep?"

What to look for in an alarm app

  • No snooze. Or at least a way to switch it off for good. Snooze is the easiest way back to sleep.
  • A task that needs you on your feet. Sums and puzzles wake your brain, but you can do them lying down. Tasks that move your body, like walking to another room or reaching a light switch, work better.
  • It comes back if you ignore it. Silencing the alarm should not count as getting up. A good alarm for heavy sleepers rings again until you have proved you are awake.
  • It rings when the app is closed. The alarm should be delivered by the phone's own alarm system, not depend on the app staying open overnight.
  • Light. Bright light tells your body the day has begun and makes it much harder to drift off again.
  • A reason to stay up. The first minute is hard. The next ten are where most people go back to bed. Something pleasant to do next helps more than a punishment.

Habits that help any alarm work

  • Charge your phone away from the bed, so that reaching it means standing up.
  • Keep the same wake-up time every day, including weekends.
  • Turn on the main room light or open the curtains as soon as you are up.
  • Check the phone's volume and Do Not Disturb settings before you rely on any alarm.
  • Aim for enough sleep. No alarm makes up for five hours a night.

When to get help

If you regularly sleep through alarms despite a full night's sleep, feel exhausted during the day, or have been told you snore heavily or stop breathing in your sleep, talk to a doctor. An alarm app is a tool for a habit, not a treatment.
